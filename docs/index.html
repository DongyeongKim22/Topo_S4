Progress detail (≤ 280 words)

This week I consolidated the inverse-tan normalization results on RGB CIFAR and re-centered the story around a stronger baseline. The key finding is that the shared global-λ model (E1) is already a strong clean+robustness baseline: on CIFAR-10 RGB it improves test@best-dev over raw E0 and gives a clear CIFAR-10-C gain, and on CIFAR-100 RGB it also improves both clean accuracy and CIFAR-100-C mean accuracy. This shows that the inverse-tan line itself is useful before any sample-wise controller is added.

I also analyzed an initial full CIFAR-10 RGB run of the PH-linear sample-wise controller (E4). E4 reached the highest best-dev accuracy among E0/E1/E4, confirming that PH-guided sample-wise control is viable, but its test@best-dev and CIFAR-10-C mean accuracy are still below the current E1 baseline. The main issue appears to be optimization: early in training, E4 stays close to the identity regime for many epochs before moving into a more moderate λ range.

So the current conclusion is not that E4 already wins, but that E4 remains the most promising sample-wise direction while E1 is the present strong baseline. I also clarified that the current CIFAR-10 RGB implementation applies one inverse-tan profile shared across RGB channels. Next I will rerun RGB CIFAR with per-channel inverse-tan coefficients, and next week I plan to run E0, E1, and E4 on Pathfinder and additional benchmarks with three seeds.
