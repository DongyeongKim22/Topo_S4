Sample-Adaptive Frequency Boost for State Space Models

Topo_S4 uses persistent homology to selectively boost high-frequency gradient sensitivity in SSMs, applying Sobolev-weighted amplification only to topologically stable spectral peaks rather than uniformly across all frequencies.

Research question

SSMs exhibit an implicit spectral bias: even with full spectral coverage (S4D-DFouT), high-frequency parameters receive weaker gradient signal due to input spectral decay. Uniform Sobolev correction boosts all high-frequency bins equally—including noise. Can persistent homology identify which bins to boost?

Expressivity ✓

S4D-DFouT places poles uniformly in the discrete Fourier domain, resolving the spectral coverage problem.

Learning dynamics ✗

Gradient signal at high frequencies decays with input spectrum (~ω-γ). Uniform Sobolev correction amplifies noise alongside signal.

PH-Sobolev (ours)

Apply (1+f²)β only to PH-identified support regions. Identity on noise bins. Lower gradient variance, higher SNR.

Method

PH-Sobolev filter

For each input, H0 superlevel persistence extracts stable spectral peaks. The Sobolev boost is applied only to their support regions.

H(ω; x) = 1 + w(ω; x) · ((1 + f(ω)²)β − 1)

w(ω; x) ∈ [0,1] is the persistence-weighted PH support. Off-support bins stay at identity. Applied in forward pass at every S4 layer.

γ–β guideline

The input spectral slope γ predicts the optimal boost exponent. Measured via log-log regression of the magnitude spectrum.

β* ≈ γ / 2   (search band: [γ/2, 0.75γ])

sCIFAR γ=0.70 → predicted β≈0.35–0.53, observed best β=0.50 ✓

Variance bound

Selective boosting provably reduces gradient variance:

tr(DPH Σ DPH*) ≤ tr(Duni Σ Duni*)

Holds for any PSD covariance Σ. No independence assumption needed. Confirmed empirically: PH achieves 0.036× uniform variance at β=2.0.

PH stability

Peaks with persistence πc > 2ε survive ε-perturbations (bottleneck stability theorem). Support width is controlled by α ∈ [0,1] with nested filtration:

α2 ≥ α1 ⇒ Sc(α2) ⊆ Sc(α1)

This week's progress

This week I transitioned from the backward-only gradient boost to a forward-consistent formulation, where the PH-Sobolev filter is applied in the forward pass and autograd produces consistent gradients. Variance sweeps confirmed that boost_layer=all is necessary (last-layer-only has no measurable effect) and that PH-persistence beats uniform at every β tested (0.25–2.0), with variance ratios as low as 0.036×. I measured spectral slopes across three LRA tasks—sCIFAR (γ=0.70), SC35 (γ=1.08), Pathfinder (γ=0.30)—and derived a β≈γ/2 guideline. On sCIFAR, the best config (α=0.6, β=0.5) achieved 90.24/89.27 dev/test vs 90.06/89.18 baseline, a modest gain consistent with this task's low high-frequency relevance. The paper draft now includes the variance proposition, SNR corollary, PH stability corollary, nested-support lemma, and backward-only non-integrability result. SC35 and Pathfinder sweeps are running to validate the γ-based cross-task prediction.

Interpretation

Selective > uniform

Gradient variance analysis and end-to-end training both confirm: PH-selective boosting outperforms uniform boosting. Uniform provides no gain over baseline on sCIFAR.

γ predicts effect size

The spectral slope γ predicts both the optimal β and the expected improvement magnitude. SC35 (γ=1.08) is the key validation target.

Two conditions needed

The method helps when (1) γ is large (gradient deficit exists) AND (2) the task requires high-frequency information. Either alone is insufficient.
